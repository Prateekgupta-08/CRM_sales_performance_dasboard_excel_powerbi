# 📊 CRM Sales Performance Dashboard | Power BI

**Giving a B2B hardware sales team full pipeline visibility for the first time — turning 11,000+ CRM opportunity records into an executive-ready performance dashboard.**

---

## 📌 Business Problem

MavenTech, a B2B computer-hardware seller, had just rolled out a new CRM to log sales opportunities. The problem: **the data lived and died inside the CRM platform** — no reporting layer, no trend view, no way for management to see how the sales org was actually performing without manually digging through opportunity records deal by deal.

Leadership couldn't answer basic but critical questions:
- Are we winning or losing more deals, and is that improving?
- Which reps and regions are actually performing?
- Which products/sectors drive the most revenue?
- Is the sales pipeline growing quarter over quarter?

**No visibility = no ability to coach reps, forecast revenue, or catch a slipping quarter before it's too late.**

---

## 🎯 What I Did

Built an interactive Power BI dashboard directly on the CRM's opportunity export, with drill-downs by region, manager, and quarter — Executive Summary, QoQ Growth, Revenue & Sales Performance, Average Deal Size, Won Deals, and Product/Sector views.

## 📊 Impact — The Numbers

*(Pulled directly from the built dashboard)*

| KPI | Value |
|---|---|
| **Total opportunities generated** | **8,800** |
| **Total deals won** | **4,238** |
| **Total deals lost** | 2,473 |
| **Closed deal value** | **$10.01M** |
| **Win rate** | **63.15%** |
| **Win/Loss ratio** | 1.71 |
| **Average deal size** | $2,361 |

### Key findings
- **Retail is the top-performing sector** by won deals (799), ahead of Technology (671) and Medical (592) — Marketing, Finance, Telecom, Entertainment, Services, and Employment trail behind.
- **Top rep, Darcel Schlecht, closed 349 won deals** — nearly 60% more than the next closest rep (Vicki Laflamme, 221) — a clear signal for best-practice sharing across the team.
- **GTX Basic is the volume driver**, accounting for ~34% of all won deals, followed by MG Special (~18.7%) and GTX Pro (~17.2%).
- **QoQ revenue swung sharply across the year:** Quarter 2 grew +171.98% over Quarter 1, before Quarter 3 (-3.37%) and Quarter 4 (-6.03%) cooled off — a pattern invisible without a trend view, and a natural trigger for a "why did momentum stall after Q2" conversation with sales leadership.
- **Average deal size varies far more by *product* than by sector** — most sectors cluster around $2K–$3K average deal size, while specific product lines (like MG Advanced) command notably higher average tickets, pointing to where premium positioning is working.

## 🧭 How This Drives Business Decisions

| Business Question | Dashboard Answer | Decision It Enables |
|---|---|---|
| Is the sales team winning enough? | 63.15% win rate, 1.71 win/loss ratio | Set realistic quota and forecast targets grounded in actual conversion, not guesswork |
| Who should mentor other reps? | Darcel Schlecht wins 58% more deals than the #2 rep | Formalize a best-practice/shadowing program built around the top performer |
| Which sector to prioritize for account expansion? | Retail leads at 799 won deals | Direct sales development/account management effort toward Retail-sector prospects |
| Why did growth stall in H2? | QoQ growth went from +172% (Q2) to -3.4% (Q3) to -6% (Q4) | Investigate pipeline coverage and rep capacity heading into Q3/Q4 before the next cycle |
| Which products to feature in campaigns? | GTX Basic + MG Special + GTX Pro = ~70% of won deals | Focus marketing spend and bundling offers on the products already converting |
